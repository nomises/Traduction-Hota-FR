===== Map name =====
Beyond the Horizon

===== Map description =====
To defend the town of Burton from the Ogres, you must defeat their leader Zog. If Henrietta or Frederick are defeated, the scenario will be lost. The heroes are limited to the level 12. Heroes will bring the components of the Wizard’s Well and the Ring of the Magi to the next scenario.

We had been through a lot on the way, and many of us had taken the journey very hard. And still, the moment we were on solid ground and taking in the air of the new world, the Eeofolian refugees cried out as one that they hated the place, and demanded that we keep going. I had no idea what to say to them, so I just looked at Frederick. I could see how great a toll it took on him to be forced into the company of the villagers, who kept pestering him with their ill-placed complaints and requests. The town made a strong impression, though a mixed one. There were… big houses? Small factories? Something in-between the two, all over the place. Smoke, steam, creaking wood and clanking metal... Nothing like our quiet, tidy villages, but I felt my compatriots would settle in quickly. There was no way out, and if a halfling put down roots somewhere, it would soon bound to become a cozy home, even if it had to start with a burrow under a mountain. We didn't have to dig, though—Sam had quartered the refugees in old adobes on the outskirts. 		They had been built many years ago by a small tribe of ogres who had quarreled with their kin and come begging to be hired as laborers, but in the end the giants proved incapable of doing useful work and drifted away, never to be heard from again. Their dwellings had stood decaying ever since, and they were about to be taken apart for stone, before they suddenly came in handy. The little halflings thought these structures huge and uncomfortable, but only at first. In a couple of days the walls were lined with all kinds of potted tomatoes and other plants, and Urstan, the village carpenter, was busy crafting elegant tables and chairs to fit the new dwellers.		The locals, the mechanics, turned out to be people of few words, but awe-inspiringly hardworking. Even most children here were well-accustomed to getting up early and helping the adults in their shops or forges instead of horsing around and playing. Sam turned out to have a wealth of organizational talent—the very next day each halfling had been issued a leather apron with a set of simple tools and instructed on the work to be done. 		Labor is the best remedy for sad thoughts. Before I knew it, Sam's new apprentices were up to their ears in the production of all sorts of mechanical parts: gears, bushings, shafts, cams, and things of whose purpose I had not the slightest idea; I had only ever seen them piled up in a workshop in distant Eeofol. Like, what is a nozzle valve and what is it for? I could have asked Frederick, of course, but I didn't dare to distract him now. He was busy erecting an enormous hangar, or shed, or whatever on the edge of the village, seeing as our airships were in need of a thorough overhaul. Provided, of course, just an overhaul would be enough...		The lull didn't last long; one of Sam's scouts brought word that an army led by one of Zog's lieutenants had moved out towards Burton, and that they would be here soon. The locals didn't seem to be fazed by the news, but the clanging, clanking, and scraping in the shops grew louder, and the town sank into a heavy mixture of steam and smoke.

Three weeks have passed since we left Eeofol. We got used to sleeping in awkward places and positions, and we grew accustomed to the rumble of the forge hammers and the heat of the furnaces. Life was very hard, but it felt somehow as if it was becoming orderly and calm again, a change even I appreciated now. For the first time, following another exhausting day, I didn't black out as soon as my head touched the makeshift pillow. The days began to come back to me in reverse order—the awkward and funny moments of getting in step with Burton's unfamiliar lifestyle, the morning when Sam met us. 		Every day and every night of our grueling flight. I remembered being struck by the sight of the vast ocean separating Antagarich and Jadame; meanwhile, most of the halflings were so exhausted, they couldn't even begin to appreciate the breathtaking view. Waiting out the darkness, hovering over the surface of the elven lakes to give our engines rest and water. Hiding high in the clouds over the dead lands, trying to evade the huge ghost dragons, hearing their deathly howls, our hearts frozen with fear. And casting the last gaze at the receding fields and villages of our native Eeofol, almost completely obscured by smoke and the glow of fire. 		The last thing I remembered before sleep overcame me was Tavin's steely eyes, no fear in them. Just selflessness, courage and determination to hold off the demons and buy us time to escape. Oh, how we… how I need someone as fearless by my side. Could I ever become like him?

Second conversation with Frederick
Message:
For some time the sky had been a source of anxiety rather than admiration for me, yet I couldn't help but recognize that it looked particularly beautiful tonight. The myriad of stars above the moonlit stone valleys glittered like finely cut gems, only found in the treasuries of the richest princes. I must confess: I shamelessly lifted this beautiful comparison from one of the novels Frederick had almost used as fuel, lamenting having this "ballast" in his hastily packed luggage instead of some more useful reference books. 		I hadn't ever seen any of those gems myself, of course, but I couldn't have said it better myself. Sam and I sat around the fire, roasting skewered meat and bread, and I listened avidly to her incredible stories. Sam seemed to have as many of them as the stars above us. I had a couple of amusing tales from village life to tell myself, too, and Sam enjoyed them well enough, choking back a hoarse laugh every now and then. I seemed to have made a first new friend in a very long time. Frederick didn't take part in our conversation—he was busy with some research of his own. He had brought a whole crateload of mirrors, tubes, curved rulers, and other instruments to the fire, but spent most of the evening in the darkness a ways aside, occasionally running up and grabbing new glass pieces and devices. 		Sam and I watched for a while as Frederick surveyed the sky through a long pipe, like those he said seafarers used on ships to see distant land, but it wasn't too much fun, so we went back to our chat. When all the meat had been eaten and only embers remained of the fire, Frederick returned and raised a finger. Mysteriously illuminated by the faint flicker of the campfire, he said: 		“The moon! They are from the moon!”		“Who?” I asked.		“The demons. It all adds up: they live on the moon. That’s scarcely a surprise; too many dark cults and practices are associated with this luminary. Every scientist and navigator knows: our world is shaped like a sphere, and the luminaries orbit around it, giving us sunlight by day and moonlight at night. Academic science believes that only incorporeal creatures can dwell on the Sun and the Moon. There are other hypotheses, but when choosing between the improbable and the absolutely improbable, I always end up leaning towards the more down-to-earth explanation. I have been looking at the Moon through a telescope for a very long time. I am sure now that what I have been able to make out are mountains, plains and countless craters, which means that the places I see are material. The demons probably built their own cities and castles there—and surely, they were watching our world below, perhaps through a spyglass just like mine. Full of jealousy of our blue oceans, our green forests and vast meadows, they were preparing an invasion, and that night, their stone ships rained down on Eeofol, turning the fertile land into an ash-filled desert akin to the one from which they came. Those were ships—there is no doubt about that. When I looked into the cracks, I saw something there—probably not mechanisms... or mechanisms operating on principles unknown to me, but most definitely products of the mind rather than natural formations.”		That night I slept badly—I dreamt of the devils again. They tore up trees, crushed mountains into rubble with their bare hands, and stacked the rocks up into huge, ugly towers. Is there any power that can resist this?
